I am a Machine Learning Research Scientist at the Arc Institute, where I develop and evaluate large-scale models that predict how cells respond to perturbations. My work spans the full model lifecycle, from training on observational data and post-training on perturbational data to rigorous evaluation across biological contexts.

More broadly, I study how models can generalize beyond their training distributions by using structure in data, including symmetries and causal relationships. I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Purdue University, advised by Prof. Bruno Ribeiro and in close collaboration with Prof. Haggai Maron.
